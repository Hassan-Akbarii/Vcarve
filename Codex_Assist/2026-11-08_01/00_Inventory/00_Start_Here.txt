What the simulated shop does
The shop prepares flat blanks, checks moisture, fixtures and machines parts, dry assembles, glues with
cauls and a common end datum, then flattens and trims. Tabs stay in sacrificial end waste. No handles,
juice groove, inlay or hidden joinery is included.
How acceptance is defined
The practice dry-fit target is hand assembly without force and a provisional 0.10 mm feeler check.
Proposed finished tolerances are +/-0.5 mm in plan and +/-0.3 mm in thickness. Repeat three accepted
coupon pairs before claiming repeatability. The full criteria are in the client brief.
Before the real job: replace each practice assumption with the client's approved value. The assumption register in
folder 01 distinguishes supplied facts, invented exercise values and open release decisions.

Compare three B variants
Keep A nominal. Save B00 at zero allowance, B025 at -0.025 mm and B050 at -0.050 mm. A negative
outside-profile allowance makes B smaller. The whole B outline changes in this simple test; keep its
original vectors.
Cut only after operator review. Check top, middle and bottom gap, edge squareness, movement and
tear-out. Log actual results in Test_Results.csv. Then repeat three accepted pairs and finally test the full
board.
Keep three ideas separate: cutter radius compensation, temporary finishing stock and final fit allowance. A
mathematically matching curve does not establish a physically matching joint.
The last pass can engage the full 28 mm edge height; the small roughing stepdown does not prove this is safe. Use the
cutter maker's guidance and the actual operator's test plan.

Try one concrete client revision
Change width from 508 to 520 mm, keeping height 381 mm. Copy parameters.json to a new revision,
update width and revision, and run the generator into a fresh output folder. Confirm the new master
measures 520 x 381 mm.
Import the new vectors into copies of the CRV jobs. Reselect or relink geometry, recalculate, review
stock and retention, and save new setup sheets. The script does not create native CRV, toolpaths or
machine code.
One master per revision: direct VCarve edits do not update the JSON. Decide which source is authoritative before the
next change; do not regenerate over a client's manual edits.

Your ArtCAM experience is a useful starting point for stock, cutter and toolpath concepts. This exercise
helps test how quickly you can organize an editable VCarve workflow. It does not establish hands-on
hardwood CNC experience by itself.
Milestone
Evidence required
1 Digital familiarization
Open a genuine CRV, make an edit, recalculate and save a preview
2 Coupon CAM
Two material-specific native jobs, allowance variants and setup notes
3 Five-part CAM
Five editable jobs, stock schedule, job sheets and revision example
4 Physical validation
Actual coupon measurements, photos and repeated cuts
5 Real production release
Accepted full board and verified machine/controller/post/fixture
What to send a prospective client
Use the editable case-study template in folder 09. Show your own VCarve screenshots and native jobs,
explain the shared-boundary and coupon approach, and list physical cutting as pending unless
performed. A 3 to 5 minute walkthrough plan is included.
Before a firm quote
Confirm approved geometry, thickness, stock, grain, preparation, assembly, acceptance tolerances,
VCarve version, machine/controller, cutter details, workholding, who runs tests, feedback timing and the
prototype revision limit.
Role split: you can lead geometry, file organization and automation. Your collaborator should own or review
woodworking decisions. If neither person has CNC machining experience, add an experienced operator before promising
production-ready files.
Scope discipline: time the exercise first. Quote a small design-and-coupon milestone, then the full board and
machine-specific release. Do not turn tutorial files into claimed personal portfolio work.
